Your email address is the one thing you hand to every site you sign up to, and it is the thing
that ties all of those accounts back together the moment one of them leaks. Bramble can now hand
each of them a different one.

Press the alias button on an entry, or take the row that appears in the sign-up form itself, and
Bramble creates a fresh forwarding address, fills it in, and saves it with the login. The mail
arrives in your real inbox as usual. When a site starts selling the address, or turns up in a
breach, you switch off that one alias and nothing else changes.

It is in the browser extensions, the desktop app, and both phone apps.

## Where the addresses come from

Bramble is not the alias service. It uses an account you already have, or a domain you already own,
and the mail never comes near us. Three options today:

- **[Addy.io](https://addy.io)** and **[SimpleLogin](https://simplelogin.io)**. Paste an API key
  from your account into Settings and you are done. Both are free to start with, and both work the
  same way if you self-host them.
- **A domain you already own**, with no account anywhere and nothing to paste. If your mail host
  lets you point a whole domain at one inbox, and most do, then `anything@yourdomain` already
  arrives and an alias is just a name nobody has used yet. Bramble makes one up on the spot: words,
  like `quiet-fox-42`, or letters and numbers, like `k3f9x2ab7q`.

That last one is the quiet favourite. There is no key, no account, no allowance to run down, and no
waiting, because nothing has to be asked of anyone. The one thing it cannot do is warn you that you
typed the domain wrong, so give it a second look when you set it up.

More providers are on the way. Bramble is a password manager rather than an alias service, though,
so switching an alias off, changing where it forwards, or deleting it stays where those settings
already live: in your provider's own dashboard, or in your own mail host.

## Nothing happens behind your back

Setting up a provider is the on switch. Until you do, Bramble contacts nobody and the feature does
not show up anywhere in the app.

After that, an address is only ever made when you ask for one, by pressing the button. Never on
page load, never in the background, never while you are still deciding. That matters more here than
with a generated password: each alias is a real record on your own account, and some plans only
allow a handful of them.

Your API key is kept in the vault itself, encrypted like everything else in there, which is why it
follows you to your other devices and why the vault has to be unlocked to make an alias. The
[privacy policy](/privacy) has the details, alongside Bramble's other optional network features.

Grab it from [bramble.sh](https://bramble.sh), or read the
[source](https://github.com/flythenimbus/bramble). Questions welcome, as always.
